<p>Estonian airspace occasionally sees drones that should not be there. Some are simply lost or flown carelessly. Some are reconnaissance. The behaviour that matters is the same in every case: report it, keep your distance, and do not become part of the problem.</p>
<p>Not every notification you receive means the same thing, and the difference is worth learning.</p>
<p>**"Possible air threat" — stay alert**</p>
<p>If the Defence Forces inform you of a possible air threat, it is not an order to shelter. Continue your normal activities but stay observant. If you cannot see or hear a drone, keep an eye on your surroundings while you are outdoors and be ready to shelter if you notice anything. Keep your phone charged and follow official instructions.</p>
<p>**"Shelter now" or sirens — shelter immediately**</p>
<p>If the message tells you to shelter, or if the sirens sound, go inside the room you are already in. If you are outdoors, find the nearest indoor space, the lowest floor, or the best protected spot available. Indoors, keep away from windows and doors and put as many walls as possible between yourself and the outside air. Stay there until you are told the danger has passed.</p>
<p>**If you see a suspicious drone**</p>
<p>Report it to the Emergency Response Centre on 112. Even if you are not sure what you are looking at, report it anyway — a suspicion is reason enough. A suspicious flying object can also be reported to the Defence Forces through the "Ole valmis!" mobile app. Check your own safety first and act cautiously.</p>
<p>**If you find a drone that has come down**</p>
<p>Do not go near it. Treat it exactly as you would treat unexploded ordnance: any unknown object may be dangerous. Call 112, move away from the object, and warn anyone nearby. Do not let anybody else approach it. And do not post the location on social media — that draws curious crowds to the site and puts more people at risk.</p>
<p>**What to do while you wait for instructions**</p>
<p>Stay in a safe place, ideally a room with solid walls away from doors and windows, until an official announcement confirms the danger has passed. Follow up on kriis.ee, olevalmis.ee or the state information line 1247. Decisions about further instructions, how institutions will operate and what happens next always come through official channels.</p>
<p>**The simple version**</p>
<p>If you see something in the air that should not be there: report it on 112, stay away from it, and do not tell the internet where it is. If you are told to shelter, shelter. Those two habits cover almost every situation.</p>
